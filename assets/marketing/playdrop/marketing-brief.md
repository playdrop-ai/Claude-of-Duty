# Claude of Duty marketing brief

## Positioning

The strongest truthful promise is a substantial tactical browser FPS whose battlefield, weapons, materials, effects, animation, and audio are generated in code. The store presentation should lead with immediate gunplay, then reveal the procedural and Gauntlet Loop creation story.

The literal truth source is Matt Shumer's original launch gameplay video. The shipped game and repository support the deeper claims about AI soldiers, procedural systems, and the Gauntlet Loop.

## Protected and editable inputs

- Protected: `icon.png`, `hero/hero-landscape.png`, `hero/hero-portrait.png`, the game title, and Matt Shumer's identity.
- Reference only: Matt's launch video, extracted source frames, the live game, and the original repository.
- Editable: the store-video cut, four new promotional screenshot compositions, listing copy, captions, alt text, and media metadata.
- Forbidden: generated gameplay video, altered mechanics, multiplayer claims, progression claims, external assets presented as runtime content, Call of Duty branding, platform chrome, browser chrome, watermarks, and invented weapons, enemies, maps, vehicles, or rewards.

## Category research

Five successful adjacent shooters use action-first imagery, short promises, and a clearly readable weapon or target:

1. BattleBit Remastered leads with large-scale FPS combat and battlefield chaos. Its strongest presentation keeps the promise concrete and action-led.
2. Ravenfield centers solo battlefield action, recognizable opposing soldiers, guns, and physics rather than lore-heavy copy.
3. Combat Master repeatedly uses compact uppercase promises such as `NEXT-GEN GUNFIGHT` and connects speed and performance directly to visible combat.
4. Krunker makes browser immediacy, the weapon silhouette, target visibility, and instant play the central hierarchy.
5. Shell Shockers uses an instantly recognizable first-person target and a simple visual joke, proving that one legible action can carry a browser-shooter image.

The PlayDrop Starfold precedent confirms the desired listing pattern: four native 16:9 compositions, one bold two-to-four-word headline per image, distinct focal states, strong thumbnail legibility, and no explanatory body copy.

## Store video brief

- Opening proof: aiming and firing appear within the first two seconds.
- Action: move through the market, acquire a target, fire, then cut forward within the same real run to the next engagement.
- Payoff: retain both visible `ENEMY ELIMINATED` moments from Matt's real run.
- Source: Matt Shumer's 55.978-second launch video, using 00:06.500-00:14.800 and 00:22.000-00:25.800 from the same continuous run.
- Final duration: 12.1 seconds.
- Output: 1920 by 946, 60 FPS, H.264, AAC stereo 48 kHz, square pixels, no crop, no stretching, no captions, and no host or browser chrome.
- Protected audio: preserve the real synchronized procedural game audio from Matt's recording.
- Destination: PlayDrop landscape store video. The clean cut may also serve as a YouTube or X gameplay clip later, but no social package is part of this update.
